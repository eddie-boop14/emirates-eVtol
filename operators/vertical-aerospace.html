Vertical Aerospace

Vertical Aerospace is a Bristol-based eVTOL developer (NYSE: EVTL) building the four-seat Valo aircraft, the production successor to its VX4 prototype. Despite appearing on Gulf watchlists, the company has no documented presence in the United Arab Emirates: no UAE customer order or letter of intent, no vertiport agreement, and no published arrangement with the GCAA, DCAA, RTA or any Emirati government body. Its only confirmed Gulf activity sits in Saudi Arabia, through memoranda of understanding signed in February and July 2026.

About

Vertical Aerospace is a British eVTOL manufacturer headquartered in Bristol and listed on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker EVTL. In December 2025 the company unveiled Valo, a piloted four-seat aircraft that succeeds the VX4 prototype and carries the production configuration forward; company material gives it a range of up to 100 miles and a cruise of up to 150 mph, with four seats at launch and a stated path to six. References to a 'VX4' fleet in the UAE context are therefore doubly out of date: the aircraft designation has changed, and no such fleet is committed anywhere in the Emirates.

On the central question for this reference site, the answer is plainly negative. A review of Vertical Aerospace's own press releases through 28 July 2026 - including the December 2025 Valo launch, the March 2026 programme and demand update, the April 2026 transition-flight release, the 13 July 2026 business update and the Farnborough releases of 20 and 24 July 2026 - produces no mention of the United Arab Emirates, Dubai, Abu Dhabi or the GCAA. The named customers in the roughly 1,500-unit pre-order book are American Airlines, Avolon, Bristow, GOL and Japan Airlines, with more recent additions JetSetGo in India and Heli Air Monaco. None is an Emirati operator. Equally, no UAE regulator or government announcement reviewed for this entry names Vertical Aerospace among the manufacturers working in the country.

The seed note behind this entry asserted a 'Dubai focus for GCC routes' and placed the company at Al Maktoum International (DWC). Both claims are refuted. Vertical's Gulf engagement is in Saudi Arabia, not the UAE: on 22 July 2026 the company announced a memorandum of understanding with Saudi Arabia's General Authority of Civil Aviation covering type certification validation and the development of operational frameworks for advanced air mobility, alongside a separate MoU with Cluster2 Airports Company to explore eVTOL operations across its airport network. That followed a three-party MoU of 10 February 2026 with AHQ Group and the Saudi National Industrial Development Centre on industrial localisation, deployment and investment. No equivalent Emirati counterpart exists, and no company source ties Vertical to DWC or to any other UAE vertiport.

The certification picture explains part of the absence. Vertical is certifying with the UK Civil Aviation Authority in concert with EASA, under its own Design Organisation Approval, and its April 2026 two-way piloted transition was flown under direct CAA oversight. That is a slower and more centralised route than the validation-led approaches that have brought American manufacturers into Dubai and Abu Dhabi. In its 13 July 2026 update the company confirmed an expanded Permit to Fly granted on 10 July 2026 allowing public demonstration flights away from Cotswold Airport, and moved its Type Certification expectation to 2029 from 2028. It flew the first public eVTOL transition flight at Farnborough on 20 July 2026, added a third prototype, and is targeting Critical Design Review by end-2026 and hybrid-electric flight testing in the first half of 2027.

The practical conclusion for UAE readers is that Vertical Aerospace is a credible European programme with no Emirati footprint. It operates no aircraft in the UAE, holds no announced UAE order, has no vertiport arrangement, and is not party to any published GCAA or Dubai process. Its nearest partner relationship of UAE relevance is with Skyports Infrastructure - a Vertical partner on a planned UK air taxi network announced in November 2024, and separately the developer of Dubai's vertiports - but that is a shared counterparty, not a Vertical presence in the Emirates. This entry should be treated as a placeholder unless and until a named primary source records an actual UAE commitment.

Frequently asked questions

Does Vertical Aerospace have any UAE orders or customers?
No. No Vertical Aerospace press release identifies an Emirati airline, operator, lessor or government body among its roughly 1,500 pre-orders. The named customers are American Airlines, Avolon, Bristow, GOL, Japan Airlines, and more recently JetSetGo and Heli Air Monaco.
Is Vertical Aerospace operating from Al Maktoum International (DWC)?
No. That claim comes from an unverified internal note and is not supported by any company, GCAA or Dubai government source. Vertical's flight testing is conducted in the United Kingdom, including at Cotswold Airport and, on 20 July 2026, at Farnborough.
What Gulf activity does Vertical Aerospace actually have?
Saudi Arabia only. In July 2026 it signed MoUs with the General Authority of Civil Aviation on certification and regulatory framework development, and with Cluster2 Airports Company on eVTOL operations. In February 2026 it signed a three-party MoU with AHQ Group and the Saudi National Industrial Development Centre. All are memoranda of understanding, not firm orders or operating approvals.
Which regulator is certifying the Valo aircraft?
The UK Civil Aviation Authority, working with EASA, under Vertical's Design Organisation Approval. Type Certification is expected in 2029 following a revision announced on 13 July 2026. No UAE GCAA validation programme has been announced for the aircraft.
Where does the VX4 flight test programme stand in 2026?
Vertical completed its first piloted two-way transition flight in April 2026 under CAA oversight, received an expanded Permit to Fly on 10 July 2026, and flew the first public eVTOL transition flight at Farnborough on 20 July 2026. It is targeting Critical Design Review by the end of 2026 and hybrid-electric flight testing in the first half of 2027. The aircraft is now designated Valo rather than VX4.
Could Vertical Aerospace enter the UAE market later?
Possibly, but nothing is announced. The company has publicly positioned itself around UK CAA and EASA certification rather than fast-track validation routes, and it has chosen Saudi Arabia as its Gulf entry point. Any UAE entry would need a GCAA validation pathway and a named Emirati partner, neither of which exists in the public record as of July 2026.
